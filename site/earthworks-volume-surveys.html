Earthworks volume surveys

Earthworks volume surveys: cut and fill you can stand behind

We survey the ground by drone and RTK GNSS, compare it with your design or an earlier survey, and give you cut and fill quantities with a heat-map showing exactly where the ground is high and low.

What an earthworks survey is used for

  • Pricing: cut and fill from the existing ground to the design formation, before you quote or order.
  • Topsoil strip: depth and volume actually stripped, measured against the ground before the strip.
  • Muck-away and import: how much has left site or been brought in, as a check against wagon loads and tickets.
  • Platform and formation levels: how close the graded ground is to design before stone goes down.
  • Interim valuations and disputes: an independent, repeatable quantity with the data behind it.

Why measure the whole surface

Earthworks quantities built from a handful of levels on a grid are only as good as the points picked up. On a site with humps, hollows and old ground, a 20 m grid can miss thousands of cubic metres. A drone survey measures the whole surface, so every hump and hollow is in the calculation, and the heat-map shows where the volume actually is.

Every surface is fixed to ground control measured with our RTK GNSS rover and checked against points kept out of the processing. When two surveys are compared, the same control is used for both, which is what makes the difference between them reliable.

Built by someone who has run the earthworks

Dimensure was set up by a groundworks project manager with 12 years on UK housing sites. We know the questions that come up at valuation and when the muck-away bill arrives, and the reports are laid out to answer them, area by area, in the same terms as your bill of quantities.

How an earthworks survey works

  1. Drawings and purpose

    The site plan, the design surface or levels, and what the numbers are for: pricing, a strip, a valuation.

  2. Control

    Control and check points measured with RTK GNSS on your site grid or Ordnance Survey grid.

  3. Fly and level

    The site is flown, and any areas the drone can't see are levelled with the rover.

  4. Compare and report

    Surfaces compared, volumes split by area, heat-map and summary issued.

What to tell us for the best numbers

The most useful thing you can do is survey before work starts. A survey of the original ground is the baseline every later volume is measured from, and it can't be recreated once the ground has been moved. Beyond that: the design surface in LandXML or 3D DWG, the areas you want quantities split into, and any layer depths (topsoil, stone, formation) you want allowed for. We report in-place surveyed volumes; bulking and compaction factors stay with your engineer or estimator.

Questions we get asked

What is an earthworks volume survey?

A survey of the ground surface compared with another surface: the design levels, or an earlier survey of the same site. The difference between them gives the volume of cut and fill, area by area, and a colour heat-map shows where ground is high or low. It's how earthworks are priced, tracked and valued.

Can you work out cut and fill against our design?

Yes. Send the design surface (a LandXML or a 3D DWG from your engineer) or the levels drawings, and we compare the surveyed ground with it. You get cut and fill volumes, a heat-map and spot differences, and we can split the site into areas, such as roads, plots and open space, to match your bill of quantities.

Can you check how much topsoil was stripped?

Yes, if the ground was surveyed before stripping. We survey the original ground, survey again after the strip, and the difference shows the depth taken off across the whole area and the total volume. That's the fairest way to settle a topsoil strip, and the same method works for any excavation or fill.

How accurate are drone earthworks volumes?

Over a large area, very consistent. Each surface is tied to RTK GNSS control, and small level errors average out across thousands of square metres, so totals are reliable for valuations and reconciliation. On very shallow strips of 100 mm or so over small areas, the level accuracy becomes a bigger share of the depth, and we tell you when that's the case.

Do the volumes allow for bulking and compaction?

The survey measures in-place volume at the time of each survey. Bulking when material is dug and loaded, and compaction when it's placed, are factors for your earthworks engineer or estimator to apply. We state that our figures are surveyed in-place volumes so they're used the right way.

Can earthworks surveys be used for valuations?

Yes. Regular surveys measured the same way each time give an independent quantity for interim valuations, muck-away reconciliation and disputes between main contractor and groundworker, with the heat-map and data to back the numbers up.
