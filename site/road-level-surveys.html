Road & sub-base level surveys

Road and sub-base level surveys, laser scanned against design

Scan the sub-base before the binder goes down and know, across every square metre, whether it is high, low or right. A next-day report your engineer and the adoption inspector can both read.

Why scan a road layer instead of dipping it?

A dip check with a level and staff, or a GPS rover, measures a point every few metres along the centreline and channels. That is a sample. If the high spot sits between two readings, nobody finds it until the binder is down and the finished level is wrong, or until water sits in the channel after adoption.

A laser scan measures the whole layer. Hundreds of thousands of points a second, kerb face to kerb face, including the bits nobody dips: around ironwork, at tie-ins, across turning heads and at the back of footpaths. The comparison with design then runs across all of it.

What we check on a road scan

  • Sub-base or formation surface level against the design surface, with the deviation at every point.
  • Crossfall and camber across the carriageway, and channel falls towards gullies.
  • Footpath and verge levels against the back of kerb.
  • Ironwork (manhole covers, gully gratings) relative to the layer around it.
  • Kerb line and kerb upstand where it is on the drawing.

What you get back

  • A PDF report with pass/fail per section, the maximum high and low, and the percentage of the surface within tolerance.
  • A colour heat-map of the layer over the layout drawing, so the gang can go straight to the area that needs trimming or topping up.
  • Cross sections at your chainages, or every 10 m if you don't specify.
  • The registered point cloud (E57 or LAS) for your engineer, and the scan registration error so you know how tight the data is.

Tolerances: what counts as a pass

We check against the tolerance in your specification or your adopting authority's estate road guide. They differ between councils, so we always ask. As one published example, Lincolnshire County Council's estate road build tolerances are:

LayerSurface level toleranceLayer thickness tolerance
Sub-base+10 / −30 mm+40 / −0 mm
Binder course±6 mm±6 mm
Surface course±6 mm±6 mm

Example only. Your section 38 agreement or specification sets the tolerance we report against.

The tight figures are on the bituminous layers, which is exactly why the sub-base matters. Every millimetre the sub-base is high comes straight off the binder, and an under-thickness binder is a defect the adoption engineer can ask you to take up. Checking the sub-base surface is the cheapest point in the job to put it right.

How a road scan visit runs

  1. Before the visit we take your design and control, and build the design surface.
  2. On site we set up on your control or put down our own targets tied to it, then leapfrog the scanner along the road, usually every 15 to 25 metres.
  3. Same evening the scans are registered, checked and compared with design.
  4. Next working day you get the report and heat-map, ready for your engineer or the gang.

We work under your site induction and RAMS, wear the right PPE, and keep the scanner and tripod out of plant routes. Scanning is safe around people: the X9 uses an eye-safe Class 1 laser.

Where a road level survey fits in

Most contractors use a road scan at one or more of these hold points:

  1. Formation

    Confirm dig depth before the membrane and stone go in, and record the volume taken out.

  2. Sub-base

    The main check. Trim or top up before the binder crew arrives.

  3. Binder

    Record the binder before it's covered for years, and capture its thickness if we scanned the sub-base.

  4. Surface course

    Final levels and thickness for adoption, and a record if there's ever a dispute.

Want the thickness of each bituminous layer too? See tarmac depth surveys. Need a price first? Read the road level survey cost guide. Handing the road over? An as-built survey can be drawn from the surface course scan.

Already surveyed the road? If your site engineer has scanned or levelled the sub-base or binder, you don't need us on site. Send the data and the design and we'll produce the same heat-map, pass/fail and cross sections from it. See reporting from your survey data.

Questions we get asked

Can a laser scan check sub-base levels to design?

Yes. We tie the scan to your site control, compare every point on the sub-base with the design surface from your long sections, cross sections or 3D model, and report the deviation across the whole road against the tolerance you work to.

What tolerance does a road and sub-base scan check against?

Whatever your specification or adopting authority uses. As an example, Lincolnshire County Council's published estate road tolerances are +10/-30 mm on sub-base surface level and ±6 mm on binder and surface course. Tell us yours and it goes on the report.

What do you need from us for a road and sub-base scan?

The road design (long and cross sections, a 3D design surface or a DWG with levels) and two or three site control points or benchmarks we can tie into. If you only have 2D drawings with levels, we build the design surface from them.

How much estate road can a laser scanner survey in one visit?

Typically 200 to 300 metres of estate road and footpaths in a half-day, depending on bends, parked plant and how much needs scanning either side. Longer runs are done as a full day.

Does a road scan hold up the groundworks gang?

Rarely. We leapfrog the scanner along the road and work around the gang. Machines can keep working a safe distance away; we only need the section being scanned to be clear while the scanner is turning.

Is a road and sub-base scan the same as a tarmac depth survey?

It is the first half of one. If we scan the sub-base and then rescan the finished surface, the difference between the two is the thickness laid at every point. See our tarmac depth surveys page.

Is laser scanning better than a GPS rover for checking sub-base levels?

For checking a finished layer, usually yes. An RTK GPS rover measures one point at a time and is typically good to around 15 to 30 mm in height in real site conditions, which is coarse against a ±6 mm binder tolerance. A laser scan measures the whole surface kerb to kerb, so a high spot between dips still shows up. Rovers remain the right tool for setting out and day-to-day control, and many sites use both.

How accurate is a laser scan for checking road levels?

Accurate enough to check estate road tolerances, with the margin stated on every report. Our Trimble X9 is specified at 2.3 mm 3D point accuracy at 10 m, and we set up every 15 to 25 m so the surface being checked stays close to the scanner. Each report gives the registration error for that job. If the data is not tight enough for the tolerance, for example because of standing water, we say so and rescan.

Can a road scan check formation level and excavation volumes?

Yes. Scanning the formation before the membrane and stone go in confirms the dig depth against design across the whole road, and records the volume taken out. That gives you evidence for the muck-away and a check that the sub-base will finish at the right thickness. Volumes are an extra deliverable, so mention them when you book and they are included in the fixed price.

Do adoption engineers accept laser scan reports for estate roads?

They are accepted as supporting evidence, but they do not replace the adopting authority's own inspections under your section 38 agreement. The report shows level and deviation across the whole layer against the tolerance you give us, with a dated point cloud the authority's engineer can check. Many contractors use it to prove a layer before calling the inspector, so the inspection becomes a formality rather than a discovery.

Can road and sub-base scanning be done in wet weather?

Light drizzle, cold and wind are usually fine, but heavy rain and standing water are not. Water lying on a sub-base or binder distorts the readings, and a puddle in a channel hides the very low spot you are trying to find. If the weather is wrong for the check, we rebook at no charge rather than issue a pass we can't stand behind.

Can you report on a road our own engineer has already surveyed?

Yes. Send the scan or the level data with the road design, the control and your tolerance, and we compare it with design and produce the usual heat-map, pass/fail and cross sections, with no site visit. With rover or total station levels the surface between points is interpolated, and the report says so.
