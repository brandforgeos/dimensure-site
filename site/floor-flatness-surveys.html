Floor flatness surveys

Floor flatness surveys of slabs and screeds

Every square metre of the floor measured, not a few straightedge readings. We laser scan the slab or screed and show where it's high, low or out of its flatness class, before racking, flooring or finishes go down.

Get a floor survey priceFloor flatness survey prices

In short: a floor flatness survey measures how flat and level a floor is against its specification. Scanning the whole floor gives a full heat-map of deviations and a pass or fail against BS 8204 surface regularity (SR1, SR2, SR3) or the flatness class in your contract, with the areas that need grinding or levelling marked out.

What the survey reports

  • Flatness: local bumps and dips, checked as a 2 m straightedge at every position across the floor.
  • Levelness: how far the floor sits above or below its design level and how it falls across the building.
  • Pass or fail against the class in your specification, with the worst areas listed and located.
  • Remedial areas marked on the floor plan, so the grinding or levelling contractor knows exactly where to work.
  • Joints and steps at bay joints, thresholds and door openings.

Why scan instead of using a straightedge

A straightedge survey checks the floor where it's laid down, on a grid. Anything between the grid lines isn't measured. A laser scan measures the whole floor, so a hump between two straightedge positions still shows up, and the heat-map shows the whole picture at a glance.

It also leaves a dated 3D record of the floor as handed over. If racking won't sit level or tiles crack months later, there's a measured answer to whether the floor was in tolerance.

Main contractors and developers

Accept the floor on evidence, not on trust, before racking, fit-out or tenants arrive.

Warehouse and industrial clients

Check free-movement floors against their TR34 class before the racking is installed and the floor is covered.

Floor finishers

Know how much levelling compound or grinding a screed needs before you price it, instead of finding out with the tiles in your hand.

What it doesn't cover

We measure flatness and level. We don't test concrete strength, moisture content or surface hardness. Defined-movement floors for very narrow aisle racking are normally surveyed with a profileograph along the wheel tracks; if your specification calls for that, we'll say so when we quote.

On new-build housing, slab flatness is one of the checks in our plot and oversite surveys, priced per plot.

Questions we get asked

What is a floor flatness survey?

A survey of how flat and level a floor slab or screed is, checked against the tolerance in the specification. It shows where the floor is high or low and whether it meets its flatness class, so problems can be ground or levelled before racking, flooring or finishes go down.

What are SR1, SR2 and SR3?

Surface regularity classes in BS 8204 for floor screeds and slabs. They set the maximum gap allowed under a 2 m straightedge laid on the floor: 3 mm for SR1, 5 mm for SR2 and 10 mm for SR3. SR1 is for high standard floors, SR2 for normal standard and SR3 for utility floors.

Can a laser scan check a floor against a 2 m straightedge?

Yes. The scan measures the whole floor surface, so a 2 m straightedge check can be simulated at every position and direction across it, not just where someone placed a straightedge. The result is a heat-map of where the floor is out of tolerance and by how much.

Do you survey warehouse floors to TR34?

We survey free-movement floors and report them against the TR34 classification in the specification. Defined-movement floors for very narrow aisle (VNA) racking are normally surveyed with a profileograph along the wheel tracks, and we'll tell you if your specification needs that method rather than a scan.

Can you check house slabs and screeds?

Yes. For new-build plots, slab flatness is one of the checks in our plot and oversite surveys. For screeds in homes, offices and commercial units, a floor flatness survey before the flooring contractor starts shows exactly where levelling compound or grinding is needed.

When should a floor flatness survey be done?

As soon as the slab or screed can be walked on and before anything covers it or is fixed to it: racking, raised floors, resin, tiles or timber. Surveying early leaves time to put defects right while the floor contractor is still on site.
