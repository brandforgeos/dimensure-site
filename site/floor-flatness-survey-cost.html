Cost guide

Floor flatness survey cost

What a floor flatness survey costs for a domestic screed, a commercial unit or a warehouse slab, and what decides the price.

Guide price: a floor flatness survey costs from £325 for a screed in a home or small unit, from £595 for a floor up to about 2,500 m² and from £1,195 up to about 10,000 m². You get a full-floor heat-map, pass or fail against the class in your specification, remedial areas marked on plan and the point cloud. Before VAT, travel included within 50 miles of Corby.

What drives the price

Area and layout

One open warehouse floor is quick to scan. The same area split into offices, corridors and plant rooms needs far more scanner positions.

What's on the floor

The scanner measures what it can see. Pallets, materials or access equipment on the slab hide the surface behind them, so a clear floor costs less and gives a complete result.

Phasing

Surveying each bay as it's poured catches problems while the floor contractor is still working. It means more visits, which we price as one programme rather than separate call-outs.

Questions we get asked

How much does a floor flatness survey cost?

With Dimensure, a floor flatness survey costs from £325 for a screed in a home or small unit, from £595 for a floor up to about 2,500 m², and from £1,195 up to about 10,000 m². Every survey includes the full-floor heat-map, pass or fail against your specified class and the point cloud. Prices are before VAT.

Is a scanned floor survey more expensive than a straightedge survey?

Not usually per square metre, because the scanner covers a large floor quickly. And it measures the whole surface, so you don't pay for a resurvey to find the defects a grid of straightedge readings missed.

What makes a floor flatness survey cost more?

Very large floors, floors split into many rooms, columns or stored materials blocking the scanner's view, phased surveys as bays are poured, and urgent turnarounds.
