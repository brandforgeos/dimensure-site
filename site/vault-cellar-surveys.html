Vault, cellar and void surveys

Vault and cellar surveys, without disturbing the structure

A brick vault has turned up during piling or excavation, and nobody wants to touch it until the engineer knows what it is. We measure it from the existing opening by laser scan, so the design can carry on without anyone going in.

Send us a photo of the openingVault survey prices

In short: a vault or cellar survey measures a brick vault, cellar, coal vault or void from its existing openings using a laser scanner, without anyone entering it and without disturbing the masonry. You get plans, sections and levels relative to the neighbouring walls and proposed works, with measured and inferred extents clearly separated, ready for your structural engineer. From £395.

What the survey gives your engineer

  • The shape of the vault: span, rise, crown level and soffit profile, from the scan.
  • How far it runs, measured as far as it can be seen, with the last measured point marked.
  • Where it sits relative to the neighbouring building, the boundary and your proposed piles or foundations.
  • Levels on your site datum or Ordnance Survey datum.
  • Measured and inferred kept apart: anything not seen is drawn differently and labelled, with blind spots listed.
  • Images generated from the scan and photos from the opening, so the engineer can see the shape and condition of the brickwork.

Why non-intrusive matters

An old vault next to a boundary may be helping to hold up the building beside it. Digging around it, breaking into it or sending someone inside can make that worse, and nobody wants to be the one who finds out. Scanning from the opening measures what's there with nothing touching the masonry, so the engineer can design the support scheme first and decide what, if anything, needs exposing after.

Typical finds we survey

  • Brick vaults exposed during piling
  • Coal vaults under pavements and front gardens
  • Victorian and Georgian cellars
  • Culverts and old drains found in excavations
  • Wells and shafts with an open top
  • Voids behind or under existing structures

Send a photo of the opening and we'll tell you honestly whether a scan can do the job.

When a scan isn't enough

A laser scanner measures what it can see. If the far end of a vault is full of rubble, or the structure runs under soil with no opening, no scanner can measure it. In that case we'll say so before you book, and the report will show exactly where the measured data stops.

If the buried extent has to be confirmed, the usual next steps are a geophysical survey such as ground penetrating radar (GPR), or a small trial hole agreed with the engineer. Our scan gives that work a measured starting point, so any further investigation can be targeted at the end that matters instead of the whole site. Nothing extra is carried out without being agreed first.

How it works

  1. Send a photo of the opening, the site address and the deadline. We confirm whether it can be scanned and send a fixed price.
  2. Scan from outside: the scanner is set up at the opening and around the site, tied into the neighbouring walls, boundary and any pile positions set out.
  3. Report: annotated plan, critical sections and images, with measured and inferred extents and limitations stated, plus the point cloud for the engineer.

Questions we get asked

Can you survey a vault without digging it out?

Often, yes. If there's an opening, a hatch, a broken crown or an existing access, the scanner can measure the inside of the vault from there without anyone entering it and without disturbing the masonry. What it can see is measured; what it can't see is shown on the drawings as inferred or unknown, never guessed and drawn as fact.

Do you go inside the vault?

No. We don't enter confined spaces or structures whose condition is unknown. The survey is done from outside and from existing openings, which is usually exactly what's wanted when the structure may be supporting something.

Can a laser scan find the buried end of a vault?

Only if it can see it. Where the far end is visible from an opening, it's measured. Where it's hidden by rubble or soil, the drawings show the last measured point and mark the rest as unconfirmed. If the buried extent has to be proven, a geophysical survey such as GPR, or a trial hole, is the next step, and we'll say so plainly in the report.

What do you send to the structural engineer?

Plans and critical sections with dimensions and levels, the vault's position relative to the neighbouring wall, boundary or proposed piles, annotated photos and images from the scan, and the point cloud. Measured features and inferred boundaries are clearly separated, with blind spots and accuracy stated.

How quickly can you attend?

Exposed vaults usually stop a job, so we treat them as urgent. Tell us the site, a photo of the opening and the deadline, and we'll confirm the earliest date and a fixed price the same working day.

Do you survey London pavement vaults and basements?

Yes. We travel to London for specialist jobs like this; travel is added to the price. See surveys in London.
