What drives the price

Number of visits

The cost follows how often we need to be on site, which follows how the drainage gang works. Long runs opened and recorded in one go are cheaper than short sections spread over many days, so it's worth planning the recording into the drainage programme.

What the record is for

A record for the site's own files is the standard drawing and CSV. An adoption pack drawn to the authority's format, with every visit combined, takes more office time and is priced when we quote.

Notice

Visits booked a day or two ahead are the standard price. Urgent call-outs at very short notice may cost more, and we'll say so when you call.

How our prices compare

As-laid recording is usually done by a site or setting-out engineer, who in 2026 typically costs around £600 a day with equipment (THS Concepts' setting out guide price, checked October 2026). Our half-day visit includes the drawing and data, so you pay for the record, not just the time.

Included on every visit

  • RTK GNSS survey, checked to control
  • Crown and cover levels, calculated inverts
  • Geotagged photos of each section
  • As-laid drawing in DWG and PDF
  • CSV of every point
  • Fall or position issues flagged same day

Questions we get asked

How much does an as-laid survey cost?

With Dimensure, an as-laid survey costs from £325 per visit of up to four hours on site, including the RTK GNSS survey, geotagged photos and an as-laid drawing in DWG and PDF with a CSV of points. A whole phase recorded on a programme of visits is priced on the drainage layout. Prices are before VAT, with travel included within 50 miles of Corby.

Why price per visit rather than per metre?

Because what takes time is being there when the trench is open, not the length of pipe. A visit can record 200 metres of pipe or a single chamber, depending on what the gang has opened. Pricing per visit keeps it simple and lets you call us out when you need us.

Is it cheaper to have the site engineer do it?

If a site engineer is there full-time with a rover and has the time, possibly. On most housing and civils jobs the engineer is busy setting out, and as-laid records get left until the end and redrawn from the design. That's the record adopting authorities and future contractors end up relying on, and it's often wrong.

What costs extra?

Drawing up a full adoption-format as-built for a whole phase from several visits, combining our records with CCTV survey data, urgent call-outs at very short notice, and sites outside our standard travel area. All are agreed before booking.
