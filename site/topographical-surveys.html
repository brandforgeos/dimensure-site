Topographical surveys

Topographical surveys by laser scan

An accurate plan of the land before anyone designs on it. We laser scan the plot or site, draw the levels, contours, boundaries, buildings and trees your architect or engineer needs, and give you the full 3D scan as well.

In short: a topographical survey is a scaled plan of a site's levels and features, used to design buildings, extensions, roads and drainage. We produce it from a 3D laser scan, so you get the drawn plan in PDF and DWG and a point cloud of the whole site that can be measured again later.

What the survey shows

  • Ground levels and contours across the site, at the interval your designer asks for.
  • Boundaries: fences, walls, hedges and gates, with heights where they matter.
  • Buildings on and next to the site: footprints, door thresholds and floor levels, and eaves and ridge heights where the scanner can see them.
  • Trees with trunk position, canopy spread and approximate height.
  • Hard surfaces: roads, kerbs, footpaths, drives, steps and retaining walls.
  • Drainage and services on the surface: manholes and gullies with cover levels, valve covers, poles and overhead lines.

Why a scan beats a points-only survey

A traditional topo survey is only as good as the points someone chose to pick up on the day. If the architect later asks for the height of a neighbour's window or the level at the back of a wall, it's another visit. With a laser scan, everything the scanner saw is in the point cloud. Missing levels are measured from the data in the office, not by going back.

It also shows the ground as it really is between points, which is where drainage falls, retaining wall heights and excavation volumes are won or lost.

Neighbouring buildings and heights

Planning drawings often need to show a new building against the houses either side. The scanner captures elevations, window positions and roof heights of neighbouring buildings from the site, as far as they're visible, without anyone needing access to next door.

Who books topographical surveys

  • Architects and designers drawing extensions, new homes and garden rooms.
  • Self-builders and small developers buying or designing a plot.
  • Civil and structural engineers designing levels, drainage and foundations.
  • Housebuilders and contractors checking existing levels before a scheme is designed or priced.

How a topographical survey works

  1. Tell us the site

    A location plan or postcode, the area to survey and what the survey is for. Your architect's brief is ideal.

  2. Fixed price

    We confirm the price, the datum and the deliverables before booking.

  3. Scan

    We scan the site from as many set-ups as it needs to see around buildings, walls and planting.

  4. Plan

    Scaled plan in PDF and DWG, checked by our team, with the registered point cloud.

What a topographical survey doesn't cover

We survey what can be seen. Buried pipes and cables need a utility or CCTV drainage survey, and tree condition and root protection areas need an arboricultural (BS 5837) survey. Legal boundaries are not the same as the physical fences and walls we measure; if ownership is in question, that's for a solicitor or a boundary specialist. Our plan gives each of them an accurate base to work on.

Questions we get asked

What is a topographical survey?

A measured survey of a piece of land showing its shape and everything on it: ground levels and contours, boundaries, buildings, trees, walls, paths, roads and drainage covers. Architects and engineers design from it, and most planning applications for new buildings or extensions on sloping or awkward sites need one.

Do I need a topographical survey for planning?

Often, yes. Councils ask for accurate existing site plans and levels on many applications, and architects need them to show how a new building sits against its neighbours and the ground. If your architect or planning consultant has asked for a topo survey, this is it. If you're unsure, ask them what level of detail and which datum they need, or send us their brief.

What does a laser scan topographical survey include?

A scaled plan in PDF and DWG with spot levels, contours, boundaries, buildings (with eaves and ridge heights where visible), trees with spread and approximate height, walls, kerbs, surfaces, and manhole and gully covers with cover levels. You also get the registered point cloud, so anything not drawn can still be measured later without another visit.

Is a laser scan survey as accurate as a traditional topo survey?

It is usually more complete. A traditional topographical survey records individual points chosen on site and joins them up. A laser scan records millions of points over everything the scanner can see, and the plan is drawn from that. Our Trimble X9 is specified at 2.3 mm 3D point accuracy at 10 m; the accuracy of the finished survey also depends on control and registration, which we state on every survey.

Will the survey be on Ordnance Survey grid and datum?

Levels and coordinates are on a local site grid as standard, which is what most extension and garden designs need. If your architect or engineer needs Ordnance Survey National Grid coordinates and Ordnance Datum levels, tell us when you ask for a price and we'll confirm how we tie the survey in and what it adds.

Does a topographical survey show underground services?

It shows what is visible on the surface: manhole and gully covers with cover levels, valve and meter covers, and overhead lines. Buried pipes and cables need a utility or drainage survey (PAS 128 utility detection or CCTV), which a specialist can add to our plan.

How long does a topographical survey take?

Most small sites and plots are surveyed in one visit, and the drawn plan follows in three to five working days. Larger sites are programmed and priced individually.
