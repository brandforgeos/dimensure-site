Stockpile survey cost

What it costs to measure your stockpiles by drone, one-off or every month, and what moves the price.

What drives the price

Number and spread of piles

Ten piles in one compound are a single short flight. The same ten spread across a large development or two yards take longer to control and fly, and each pile still needs outlining and checking in the office.

How the base is set

Piles on a known hardstanding are quick. Piles tipped onto rough ground need more levels around the toe to build a sensible base, which takes more time with the rover. A survey of the ground before tipping starts is the cheapest way to make every later survey more accurate.

Frequency

Regular surveys on the same site reuse the same control and processing set-up, which is why a programme costs less per visit than one-off surveys.

How our prices compare

Published UK guides in 2026 put a stockpile or quarry volumetric drone survey at around £700–£3,500 (Dronedesk's UK drone survey price guide, May 2026). Construction-site stockpiles are smaller than quarries, and our prices are set to suit them, with ground control included.

Included on every stockpile survey

  • RTK GNSS ground control
  • Volume of every pile in m³
  • Tonnage with a stated density
  • Marked-up aerial image
  • Base method stated per pile
  • Results usually next working day

Questions we get asked

How much does a stockpile survey cost?

With Dimensure, a one-off stockpile survey costs from £395 per visit for up to ten piles on one site, including ground control, volumes in cubic metres and tonnage where the density is known. On a regular monthly or quarterly programme it costs from £295 per visit. Prices are before VAT, with travel included within 50 miles of Corby.

What is the typical UK price for a drone stockpile survey?

Published UK guides in 2026 put a stockpile or quarry volumetric drone survey at around £700 to £3,500, depending on the size of the site, the number of piles and the accuracy needed. Construction-site stockpiles are normally at the lower end of that range.

Is it cheaper than a traditional stockpile survey?

Usually. Measuring piles with a rover or total station means walking all over them, which is slow and unsafe on loose material. A drone covers all the piles in a single short flight, so the time on site, and the cost, is lower for anything more than a couple of small heaps.

What makes a stockpile survey cost more?

A large number of piles, piles spread over a big area or several yards, piles with no clear toe that need extra ground levels to build a base, and same-day results. Sheeted or roofed piles that need a laser scan are quoted separately.
