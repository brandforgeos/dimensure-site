Extension and loft conversion surveys

Surveys for extensions and loft conversions

Every extension and loft conversion starts with existing drawings. We scan the house and give your architect accurate plans, elevations, roof sections and ground levels, so the design fits the house you've actually got.

Get a survey priceExtension survey prices

In short: an extension or loft conversion survey measures the parts of the house your design affects and produces existing plans, elevations, a roof section with headroom, and ground levels outside, in DWG and PDF with the point cloud. It's what an architect or designer needs for planning and building regulations drawings. Extensions from £495, whole house plans from £695.

For an extension

  • Ground floor plan of the rooms the extension joins, with wall thicknesses, openings and floor level.
  • The elevation the extension attaches to, with window and door heights, eaves and gutter line.
  • Ground levels around the house: patio, lawn, paths, manhole covers and the damp course line where visible.
  • Neighbouring walls and windows close to the boundary, which matter for planning.

For a loft conversion

  • Roof section: ridge and eaves heights, roof pitch and the exact headroom available.
  • Visible structure: rafters, purlins, ceiling joists, water tanks and chimney stacks in the loft.
  • Top floor plan and stair, which decide where the new stair can go.
  • Elevations for dormers and roof windows.

Why measure instead of using old plans

Old plans show the house as it was designed. Walls get moved, floors get raised and roofs get altered, and none of that is on them. Designing from them is the most common reason an extension needs redrawing halfway through building regs.

If your architect has a measuring brief, send it and we'll survey to it.

Garage conversions, knock-throughs and garden rooms

The same survey works for garage conversions (the garage, the house wall it joins and the floor levels either side), knock-throughs (the rooms involved and the wall being removed) and garden rooms (the garden levels and boundaries, which is where a garden survey comes in).

What it doesn't cover

We measure what's visible. We don't design extensions, check structure or make planning applications. For sloping floors or cracks in the existing house, see floor level surveys. For every room of the house, see measured building surveys.

Questions we get asked

What survey do I need before designing an extension?

Existing drawings of the part of the house the extension joins: the ground floor plan of the affected rooms, the rear or side elevation, and the ground levels outside. Your architect uses them for the planning and building regulations drawings. Most extension surveys don't need the whole house.

What survey do I need for a loft conversion?

A section through the roof showing ridge height, eaves, rafter and ceiling joist positions where visible, and the headroom available, plus the top floor plan, the stair position and the elevations. Headroom is usually the deciding factor in whether a loft conversion works, so it needs measuring accurately rather than estimating.

Can you tell me if there's enough headroom for a loft conversion?

We measure the exact headroom from the top of the ceiling joists to the underside of the ridge and along the roof slope. Whether that's enough depends on the new floor build-up, the stair and the regulations your designer is working to; they make that call from our measurements.

Do you include the ground levels outside?

Yes, for extensions. The levels of the garden, paths and drains around the house decide the new floor level, steps and drainage, so we capture them as part of the survey.

Do I need a survey if I already have the original plans?

Usually, yes. Original plans show the house as designed, not as built, and rarely show later changes. A survey avoids the most common extension problem: a design that doesn't match the house it's attached to.
