Cost guide

Wall verticality survey cost

What it costs to have a leaning or bulging wall measured, and what monitoring costs on top.

Guide price: a wall verticality survey costs from £325 for one wall, with a deviation map, sections and the point cloud. Several walls, whole buildings and monitoring programmes are quoted. Before VAT, travel included within 50 miles of Corby.

SurveyGuide priceTypical job
One wallfrom £325Leaning garden wall, gable end or retaining wall
Several walls or a whole buildingquotedAll elevations, or every wall on a boundary
Monitoring programmequotedBaseline plus repeat surveys at your engineer's interval

Guide prices before VAT. Every quote is fixed before we book.

What decides the price

Length and height

A short garden wall needs a couple of scanner positions. A long boundary or a three-storey gable needs more, from further back, to see the whole face.

Both sides or one

Scanning both faces shows the thickness and the full shape of the wall, but needs access to both sides, sometimes through a neighbour's property.

Vegetation

Ivy and shrubs hide the wall face. Cutting back growth before we arrive, where that's safe and allowed, gives a more complete survey.

Included

  • Laser scan of the wall face
  • Colour deviation map
  • Sections through the worst areas
  • Reference points for monitoring
  • Registered point cloud

Questions we get asked

How much does a wall verticality survey cost?

With Dimensure, a survey of one wall, such as a garden wall or a gable end, starts from £325, including the deviation map, sections through the worst areas and the point cloud. Several walls, whole buildings and monitoring programmes are quoted. Prices are before VAT, with travel included within 50 miles of Corby.

How much do repeat monitoring surveys cost?

Repeat surveys are quoted as a programme. They use the same reference points and report layout as the baseline, so they're quicker than the first visit.

Is an engineer's report included?

No. We provide the measurements, deviation map and sections. A structural engineer interprets them, and we can send the data straight to yours.
