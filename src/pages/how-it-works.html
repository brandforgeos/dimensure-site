How it works

How laser scanning works, from booking to report

What we need from you, what happens on site, how accurate the result is, and what you get back.

  1. Tell us the job

    Site or address, what needs checking, the drawings, the tolerance and when you need it. An email or the contact form is fine.

  2. Fixed price, booked date

    We confirm exactly what we'll check, what you'll get back and the price, and book a date around your programme.

  3. Scan on site

    We sign in, tie into your control where needed, and scan, working around the gang and the programme.

  4. Process and check

    Scans are registered and compared with your design, then our team goes through the results, sense-checks them against the drawings and the site, and flags any problems.

  5. Report and data

    Next working day for site QC; three to five working days for drawn surveys. Data is kept for at least six years.

What we need from you

  • Roads: long and cross sections or a 3D design surface, plus the location and coordinates of two or three control stations.
  • Plots: house-type drawings, the drainage layout showing collar positions, and plot numbers.
  • Buildings and gardens: the address, what the survey is for, and which rooms or areas you need.
  • All jobs: the tolerance or level of detail you need, site rules, induction requirements and access.

On site

On construction sites we work under your induction, permits and RAMS, wear full PPE and keep the scanner out of plant routes. The scanner levels and calibrates itself at every set-up, so there's no time lost between positions. It uses a Class 1 eye-safe laser, so nobody needs to stop work or look away.

In homes we need access to the rooms or garden being surveyed. Most homes and gardens are surveyed in a single visit, at a time that suits you.

Accuracy: what the numbers mean

In short: the Trimble X9 is specified at 2 mm range accuracy and 2.3 mm 3D point accuracy at 10 m. The accuracy of a finished survey also depends on how well the scans are joined and tied to control, so every report states that registration error for the job.

Accuracy is the reason we keep set-ups short on QC work. Points close to the scanner are the most accurate, so on a road or slab we set up often enough that the surface being checked is always within a comfortable distance.

We also tell you when the data isn't good enough for the question. A check against a ±6 mm tolerance needs tighter registration than a garden plan does. If a job can't meet the tolerance, for example because there's water lying on the surface, we say so and rescan rather than issuing a pass we can't stand behind.
