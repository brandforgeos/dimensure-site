Extensions, lofts and conversions

Most of our work in Bletchley starts with a house that's about to change. A measured building survey gives the architect exact plans, elevations and roof sections, which is what a loft conversion or rear extension is designed from. Where the plan is to split a house into flats, a lease plan survey draws each flat to HM Land Registry's requirements with its floor area.

Floors and walls that have moved

Older terraces and some post-war construction can show their age in sloping floors, sticking doors and cracked walls. A floor level survey maps every floor in the house so a structural engineer can see the pattern, and a wall verticality survey measures how far a wall leans or bulges. Both can be repeated to show whether anything is still moving.

Commercial and industrial

Around the town centre and the industrial estates, we provide lease plans and floor areas for shops and offices, and floor flatness surveys for unit and warehouse floors before racking goes in.

Gardens, plots and new estates

For garden redesigns and small developments, a garden survey or topographical survey gives levels and boundaries. On new estates such as Newton Leys we offer the full construction range, from road level checks to pre-adoption road surveys.
