What we measure

  • Walls: lengths, heights, and how far each one leans or bows along a run.
  • Corners: the real angle, so a worktop template isn't the first time anyone finds out it's not 90°.
  • Floor and ceiling: how far they're out of level across the room, for plinths, wall units and tall housings.
  • Openings: windows, doors, reveals, sill and head heights.
  • Services: visible sockets, switches, pipes, boxing, the soil stack and extract positions.
  • Stairwells: rise, opening, headroom and the walls either side.

Why trades are switching to scans

The usual site measure is a tape, a laser distance meter and a sketch. It records the lengths someone thought to measure, at the height they held the tape, and misses everything else. Then the worktop doesn't sit against the wall, the end panel needs scribing by 20 mm at the top and nothing at the bottom, or the wall units step at the ceiling.

A scan measures every surface in the room in about an hour. If a question comes up during design, the answer is already in the point cloud: no second visit, no guessing.

Kitchens in extensions and new builds

If the kitchen is going into an extension, the best time to scan is when the plaster is on and before the floor finish goes down. You get the room as built, not as drawn, and any difference between the two shows up before units are made. For whole houses, extensions and lofts, see measured building surveys and extension and loft conversion surveys.

What it doesn't cover

We measure what can be seen. Pipes and cables inside walls or under floors aren't in the scan unless they're exposed. We don't design kitchens or advise on gas, electrics or structure.
