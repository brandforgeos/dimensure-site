What a lease plan from us includes

  • A floor plan of the demise drawn to a stated metric scale, with the let area clearly edged.
  • A location plan showing the building in relation to roads and neighbouring property, so it can be identified on the ground.
  • A north point and scale bar, and the floor level of each part of the demise.
  • Included and excluded areas, such as bin stores, parking spaces, gardens or shared landings, shown as your solicitor instructs.
  • Floor areas to RICS conventions, on the plan or as a separate schedule.

Old plans cause new problems

Many lease plans in circulation were traced from old drawings or sketched from estate agents' floor plans. They're often not to scale, show walls that have moved, or leave out the garden, the parking space or the loft. That's how disputes start, and it's why HM Land Registry sends back applications with plans it can't use. Measuring the property properly before the lease is signed costs far less than fixing it afterwards.

Who books lease plan surveys

Landlords and developers

Granting new leases on flats in a converted building, or on units in a new development, with one consistent set of plans for every demise.

Solicitors and conveyancers

A plan that will go through HM Land Registry first time, supplied as a clean PDF ready to attach.

Leaseholders extending a lease

When the original plan is wrong or unclear, a new measured plan stops the problem being carried into the extended lease.

Commercial agents

Accurate NIA or GIA figures for marketing and rent reviews, measured rather than estimated.

What it doesn't cover

We measure and draw. We don't draft the lease or give legal advice on what the demise should include; your solicitor tells us that, and we draw it. For full existing plans, elevations and sections of a building, see measured building surveys.
