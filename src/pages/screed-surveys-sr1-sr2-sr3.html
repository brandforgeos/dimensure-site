SR1, SR2 and SR3 screed surveys

What the surface regularity classes actually mean, how a screed is tested against them, and why scanning the whole floor beats a handful of straightedge readings when there's a dispute.

In short: SR1, SR2 and SR3 are the surface regularity classes in BS 8204 for screeds and floors. They limit the gap under a 2 m straightedge to 3 mm, 5 mm and 10 mm respectively. A laser scan survey checks the whole screed against its class, not just a few positions, and marks exactly where it needs grinding or levelling. Surveys start from £325.

The three classes

ClassMax gap under 2 m straightedgeTypical use
SR13 mmHigh standard: thin sheet and tile finishes, demanding uses
SR25 mmNormal standard: most offices, shops and homes
SR310 mmUtility: plant rooms, stores, floors where finish is less critical

Your specification sets the class. Check it before the screed is laid, not after.

Flatness isn't the same as level

The SR classes are about local flatness: how much the surface rises and falls over a couple of metres. They don't control whether the whole floor is at the right height, or falls steadily from one end to the other. That's a separate levelness tolerance, usually given as a plus-or-minus from datum. A screed can pass SR1 and still be 15 mm low at one end, which matters at doorways and thresholds. Our surveys report both.

How a screed is usually checked

Traditionally, someone lays a 2 m straightedge on the floor at chosen positions and measures the biggest gap under it with a slip gauge. It works, but it only tests where the straightedge was placed.

How we check it

We laser scan the whole screed, then simulate a 2 m straightedge at every position and in every direction across the floor. The result is a map of every place the screed fails its class, and by how much.

When a screed survey pays for itself

Before the floor finish goes down

Tiles, sheet vinyl, resin and engineered boards all show a poor screed. Knowing exactly where it's out lets the floor layer price the levelling properly instead of finding out as they go.

When the screed is disputed

If the floor layer says the screed isn't to SR2 and the screeder says it is, a full-floor survey ends the argument with evidence both sides can see.

At handover

A dated record that the floor met its class when it was handed over, kept with the project files.
