For an extension

  • Ground floor plan of the rooms the extension joins, with wall thicknesses, openings and floor level.
  • The elevation the extension attaches to, with window and door heights, eaves and gutter line.
  • Ground levels around the house: patio, lawn, paths, manhole covers and the damp course line where visible.
  • Neighbouring walls and windows close to the boundary, which matter for planning.

For a loft conversion

  • Roof section: ridge and eaves heights, roof pitch and the exact headroom available.
  • Visible structure: rafters, purlins, ceiling joists, water tanks and chimney stacks in the loft.
  • Top floor plan and stair, which decide where the new stair can go.
  • Elevations for dormers and roof windows.

Why measure instead of using old plans

Old plans show the house as it was designed. Walls get moved, floors get raised and roofs get altered, and none of that is on them. Designing from them is the most common reason an extension needs redrawing halfway through building regs.

If your architect has a measuring brief, send it and we'll survey to it.

Garage conversions, knock-throughs and garden rooms

The same survey works for garage conversions (the garage, the house wall it joins and the floor levels either side), knock-throughs (the rooms involved and the wall being removed) and garden rooms (the garden levels and boundaries, which is where a garden survey comes in).

What it doesn't cover

We measure what's visible. We don't design extensions, check structure or make planning applications. For sloping floors or cracks in the existing house, see floor level surveys. For every room of the house, see measured building surveys.
