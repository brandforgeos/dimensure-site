What goes on the as-laid record

  • Pipe runs with crown levels at intervals and at every change, and calculated inverts from the pipe size.
  • Chambers and manholes: centre positions, cover levels and the pipes in and out.
  • Fittings: bends, junctions, connections, rodding eyes and lateral positions for each plot.
  • Ducts and utilities: position, depth below finished level and which service is which.
  • Crossings and clashes with other services, with the clearance between them.
  • Geotagged photos of every section before it's covered.

Why record before backfill

Drainage and ducts are where as-built drawings are least reliable. Runs get moved on site to miss a service or a tree root, depths change, laterals go in where the plot drainage actually came out. Months later, someone redraws the design and calls it the as-built. Then the adoption inspection, a blocked lateral or a new connection finds the truth.

A measured record taken in the open trench, with photos, removes the guesswork. It also protects the groundworker: if a level or a fall is ever questioned, there's a dated record of what was laid.

Check the falls while you still can

Because the levels are recorded as the pipe goes in, we can flag a run that's flatter or steeper than the design, or a backfall, the same day, while it's still cheap to put right.

Typical uses

  • Section 104 sewers and Section 38 highway drainage for adoption packs.
  • Street works records of what was laid in the highway.
  • Private drainage, soakaways and attenuation on housing and commercial sites.
  • Utility ducts for power, telecoms and EV charging.
  • Land drains and water supply pipes on farms and estates.

How as-laid recording works

  1. Drawings and control

    The drainage or services layout and the site control, so the record lines up with your design.

  2. Call-out or programme

    We agree how you'll tell us a run is ready and book visits around the drainage gang.

  3. Record in the trench

    Positions, levels and photos taken while the gang carries on further along.

  4. As-laid drawing

    DWG, PDF and CSV within two working days, with any fall or position issues flagged straight away.

What an as-laid survey doesn't cover

We record what's visible in the open trench. Services already backfilled need tracing by a utility survey, and the inside condition of a pipe needs a CCTV survey. We don't enter trenches that aren't properly supported or battered; recording is done from the surface with the rover pole, so safe trench support is the site's responsibility. Invert levels calculated from crown levels are labelled as calculated.
