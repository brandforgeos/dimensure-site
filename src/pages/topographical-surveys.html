What the survey shows

  • Ground levels and contours across the site, at the interval your designer asks for.
  • Boundaries: fences, walls, hedges and gates, with heights where they matter.
  • Buildings on and next to the site: footprints, door thresholds and floor levels, and eaves and ridge heights where the scanner can see them.
  • Trees with trunk position, canopy spread and approximate height.
  • Hard surfaces: roads, kerbs, footpaths, drives, steps and retaining walls.
  • Drainage and services on the surface: manholes and gullies with cover levels, valve covers, poles and overhead lines.

Why a scan beats a points-only survey

A traditional topo survey is only as good as the points someone chose to pick up on the day. If the architect later asks for the height of a neighbour's window or the level at the back of a wall, it's another visit. With a laser scan, everything the scanner saw is in the point cloud. Missing levels are measured from the data in the office, not by going back.

It also shows the ground as it really is between points, which is where drainage falls, retaining wall heights and excavation volumes are won or lost.

Neighbouring buildings and heights

Planning drawings often need to show a new building against the houses either side. The scanner captures elevations, window positions and roof heights of neighbouring buildings from the site, as far as they're visible, without anyone needing access to next door.

Who books topographical surveys

  • Architects and designers drawing extensions, new homes and garden rooms.
  • Self-builders and small developers buying or designing a plot.
  • Civil and structural engineers designing levels, drainage and foundations.
  • Housebuilders and contractors checking existing levels before a scheme is designed or priced.

What a topographical survey doesn't cover

We survey what can be seen. Buried pipes and cables need a utility or CCTV drainage survey, and tree condition and root protection areas need an arboricultural (BS 5837) survey. Legal boundaries are not the same as the physical fences and walls we measure; if ownership is in question, that's for a solicitor or a boundary specialist. Our plan gives each of them an accurate base to work on.
