What a roof survey covers

  • Roof coverings: tiles and slates, slipped, cracked or missing, and moss or growth.
  • Ridges, hips and valleys: mortar gaps, loose ridge tiles, dry-fix systems and valley linings.
  • Chimneys: stacks, pointing, pots, cowls and lead flashings.
  • Flat roofs and dormers: felt or membrane, edges, upstands and cheeks.
  • Rainwater goods: gutters, outlets and downpipes, blockages and damage.
  • Measured roof report (optional): areas, pitches and lengths for every slope.

Why look before you scaffold

Most roof problems start with a stain on a ceiling or a tile in the garden, and the first instinct is to get someone up there. Scaffolding to find out what's wrong can cost more than the repair. A drone shows the whole roof close up in a short visit, so the roofer you call already knows what needs doing, and you can compare quotes for the same job.

Because we don't do roofing work, the report is impartial. It describes what can be seen and how urgent it looks, without anything to sell.

Measured roofs for pricing and solar

The same flight can build a scaled 3D model of the roof, tied to measured points on the ground. From it we take the area and pitch of each slope, the length of every ridge, hip, valley, eave and verge, and the position of dormers, chimneys and roof lights, so re-roofing and solar panel layouts can be priced from real numbers.

Who books roof surveys

  • Homeowners with a leak, after a storm, or before selling.
  • Buyers who want a closer look than a home survey can give.
  • Landlords and property managers planning maintenance.
  • Roofers and solar installers pricing without going up.
  • Commercial and farm owners with large or fragile roofs.

  4. Report

    Marked-up photos and condition notes, plus measurements if ordered, within two working days.

What a drone roof survey doesn't cover

The drone sees the outside of the roof. It can't see inside the loft, the condition of battens, felt or timbers, or exactly where water is getting in, so a roofer may still need to look from inside. It isn't a structural survey or a home buyer's survey. Heavy rain and strong wind stop flying, and a few properties close to airports need permission first, which we check when we quote.
