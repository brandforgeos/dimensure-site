Vaults, cellars and voids

Victorian and Georgian London was built over coal vaults, cellars, wells and drains, and they're rarely on any drawing. When one is exposed during piling or excavation next to a neighbouring building, the engineer needs its extent and levels before the support scheme can be finished, without anyone disturbing it. Our vault and cellar surveys measure it by laser scan from the existing opening, and keep what was measured separate from what is inferred.

Movement in older buildings

London's terraces and mansion blocks move: floors drop, party walls lean, bays bulge. A floor level survey maps every floor as contours for the engineer, and a wall verticality survey maps a wall face against vertical. Both can be repeated to show whether movement is continuing, which is often what an insurer or a party wall surveyor needs to know.

Point clouds and lease plans

For architects and engineers who draw their own, a point cloud survey gives a registered scan of a building in E57, RCP or LAS. For landlords splitting buildings into flats, a lease plan survey produces scaled plans to HM Land Registry's requirements with floor areas.
