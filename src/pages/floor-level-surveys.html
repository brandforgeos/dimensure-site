What the survey shows

  • Spot levels across every room on a regular grid, plus at doorways, corners and wall lines.
  • Contours drawn over the floor plan, so the shape of the floor is clear at a glance.
  • The overall fall of each floor: high point, low point and the difference between them.
  • Steps and changes between rooms, at thresholds and where an old house meets a later extension.
  • A dated record that can be repeated months later to show whether anything is still moving.

Why scan rather than use a level

A traditional level survey takes readings at points someone chose on the day. A scan measures the whole floor surface, so the contours come from millions of points, not a few dozen. Nothing between the readings gets missed, and the same scan gives wall positions and room sizes for the floor plan underneath.

Who uses floor level surveys

Structural engineers

Order the levels, get a contour plan and the point cloud, and spend your site visit assessing rather than measuring. We can survey to your grid and spacing if you have a standard brief.

Homeowners and buyers

If a floor feels wrong, measuring it is the first step. You'll get a plain plan showing how far out it is, which you can take to an engineer or surveyor for advice on what it means.

Loss adjusters and insurers

A dated, repeatable record of floor levels at the start of a claim, which can be surveyed again in the same way to monitor movement.

Architects and builders

Existing floor levels at every door and threshold before an extension, loft or knock-through is designed, so steps and ramps are planned on paper, not found on site.

What it doesn't cover

We measure. We don't diagnose the cause of movement or say whether a building is structurally safe; that's for a chartered structural engineer or building surveyor, and we're happy to work to their brief. For new screeds and slabs checked against a flatness class, see floor flatness surveys and our guide to SR1, SR2 and SR3 screed surveys. For leaning or bulging walls, see wall verticality surveys.
