What you get

  • A registered point cloud: every scan position joined into one model, with the registration checked, not just accepted.
  • Cleaned data: people, passing cars and obvious noise removed, so you're not drawing round ghosts.
  • Your format: E57 for anything, RCP/RCS for Autodesk, LAS/LAZ for terrain and GIS work.
  • A scan position plan showing where the scanner stood and what it could and couldn't see.
  • Optional OS grid tie-in using our RTK GNSS rover, so the cloud drops straight onto a site plan or topo.

Who books a point cloud on its own

Architects and architectural technicians who prefer to draw existing plans and elevations in their own template. Structural engineers checking spans, deflections and wall positions. Interior designers, kitchen and joinery firms who want every wall and ceiling as it really is, not as the plans say. Contractors planning temporary works, crane positions or a tight install.

It also suits anyone who needs a record of something before it changes: a building before strip-out, a structure exposed for a short time, or a site before work starts.

Point cloud or full drawings?

Choose a point cloud if you or your designer will draw from it, or you just need a record.

Choose a measured building survey if you want finished floor plans, elevations and sections in DWG and PDF.

Every measured and topographical survey we do includes the point cloud anyway, so you never lose the data.

How the scan is done

We set the scanner up at enough positions to see every surface you need, inside and out. Each set-up captures a full sphere of measurements in a few minutes. Back in the office the scans are registered together using the overlap between them, and the registration report is checked so you know how well the positions fit.

Where the cloud needs to be on Ordnance Survey grid and height datum, we observe control with the RTK GNSS rover while we're on site and fix the cloud to it. Otherwise it's on a clean local grid, levelled, with a known origin.

What a scanner can't see

A laser scanner measures surfaces in its line of sight. Anything behind furniture, inside closed cupboards or above a ceiling isn't in the cloud unless it's opened up. Very dark, wet, glossy or glass surfaces return thin or noisy data. We'll tell you before we book if any of that matters for your job, and the scan position plan shows where the gaps are.
